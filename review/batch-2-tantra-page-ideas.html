Batch 2 (Tantra company page): 10 ideas for approval

Pick 5: 3 carousels (K1 to K6) and 2 images (K7 to K10). Nothing is drafted until you pick. - Voice: the company page writes as "we" to "you". "Tantra" appears at most once per post. - Proof: product claims come only from the code-verified ground truth (PR rows). - Samples: every row is unused (batch 1 used AB35, AB30, HD12, AV41 and AB21). No row repeats. - Design systems: assigned after your pick, so your 5 get 5 different ones.

Carousels

K1. 5 Checks Every Email Passes Before It Sends

  • Hook: Most cold email trouble starts with a send that should never have gone out. Here are the 5 checks every email passes first.
  • Summary: One step per page, as a numbered stepper:
  • a do-not-contact check
  • per-mailbox caps (50 a day and 25 an hour by default)
  • a sending window in the recipient's time zone
  • automatic suppression on a hard bounce
  • one-click unsubscribe

It ends on a table of each check and the problem it prevents. - Stage: TOFU · Claims: PR-04, PR-05 - Design: Harry Dry #48, "two-tier stepper grids; final Basic/Advanced/PRO comparison table". Craft: "dark-mode variant... blue numbered-bar stepper". - Runner-up: Austin Belcak #33

K2. Warm-Up, Day by Day: From 5 Emails a Day to 100

  • Hook: A new mailbox shouldn't send 100 emails on day one. Ours starts at 5 a day and adds 5 every 3 days. Here's the whole ramp.
  • Summary: One identical page per milestone: day 1 at 5 a day, then 10, 25, 50, up to about day 57 at 100. The colour shifts from caution to go as the mailbox earns volume. The warm-up exchanges mail between your own mailboxes, never a shared pool.
  • Stage: TOFU · Claims: PR-06, PR-02
  • Design: Harry Dry #39, "identical template stamped per slide, color = judgment". Its teardown says "Template, intentionally so: repetition is the message".
  • Runner-up: Harry Dry #43

K3. Your LinkedIn Plan Sets Your Outreach Ceiling

  • Hook: A free LinkedIn account gets about 3 personalised connection notes a month. That isn't an outreach channel. Here's what each plan allows.
  • Summary: Two-row comparisons, free vs Premium vs Sales Navigator: invites a day and a week, notes, profile views, messages. It ends on the defaults we set per account (80 requests a day, 180 a week), which you can lower.
  • Stage: TOFU · Claims: PR-11, PR-12
  • Design: Harry Dry #26, "rigid two-row before/after grid, clean cards, generous margins". Craft: "headline set inside a fake OS UI element".
  • Runner-up: Harry Dry #21

K4. One Sequence, Three Channels, One Stop Rule

  • Hook: Email on day 1, a LinkedIn visit on day 2, a WhatsApp message on day 4. When a reply is detected, the sequence stops for that contact.
  • Summary: A tutorial walkthrough, one page per step, each with a real product screenshot:
  • build a sequence across Gmail, LinkedIn and WhatsApp
  • each step sends from its own account, within that account's limits
  • when a reply is detected, the sequence stops for that contact

It also covers what needs a Google Workspace admin and what doesn't. - Stage: MOFU · Claims: PR-01, PR-02, PR-16 - Design: Austin Belcak #34, "every slide pairs copy with a product UI screenshot". Craft: "annotated real-product screenshots". - Runner-up: Patrick James Cumming #42

K5. AI Personalisation on Your Own Key

  • Hook: No AI credits, no markup. One model call per email, billed by your own provider. And here's exactly when it falls back to your template.
  • Summary:
  • what gets personalised: email subject and body, and calendar invitation descriptions
  • what stays a template: LinkedIn and WhatsApp steps
  • the fallback cases: a free-email address with no website, an unreadable site, a provider error

It's shown on an annotated product screenshot. - Stage: MOFU · Claims: PR-09, PR-10 - Design: Harry Dry #31, "left-text / right-object; 'INTRODUCING' slide centered with annotated UI screenshot + hand-drawn callout arrows". - Runner-up: Harry Dry #45

K6. Three Days, One Working Engine

  • Hook: Most workshops leave you with notes. This one leaves you with a working engine: offer, pages, sequences and an AI agent running them.
  • Summary: Three posters, one per day:
  • Day 1: the 8 decisions
  • Day 2: every page, email and automation
  • Day 3: an AI agent in charge

A progress bar runs from decisions to a live funnel. It ends on what the seat includes and the next cohort date. - Stage: BOFU (CTA) · Claims: CO-02 to CO-08 - Design: Harry Dry #19, "ToFu/MoFu/BoFu three-poster row with hand-drawn spectrum bar". - Runner-up: Austin Belcak #29

K8. We Never Track Opens

  • Hook: Open tracking puts a hidden pixel in every email. We don't add one. Ever. Here's what we track instead, and why.
  • Summary: One colour-coded sentence with a pill legend: no open pixel; click tracking off by default; replies and RSVPs counted; your own mailboxes, never a shared pool. The caption covers why an open count is a weak signal next to a reply.
  • Stage: TOFU · Claims: PR-08, PR-02, PR-01
  • Design: Austin Belcak #7, "editorial diagram: color-coded semantic sentence with a pill legend". It replaces Harry Dry #5, whose teardown says "near-impossible to templatize".
  • Runner-up: Alex Vacca #40

K10. A Daily Domain Report, Scored Out of 100

  • Stage: MOFU · Claims: PR-07, PR-05, PR-04
